*For anyone who asks for, commissions or signs off teaching. You don't have to answer every row yourself. You do need to know that someone can, usually your education lead. If nobody can, you've found the gap.*

Asking for a training day is easy. Working out who needs to change what, why they don't already, and what will help them keep doing it is hard. Education standards in the NHS, in universities and in workplace learning ask the same questions. That isn't because they like paperwork. It's because the job really is complex.

**New to this?** Read [the bigger picture](/facilitator/before-you-plan#the-bigger-picture) first. It takes five minutes.

> **What the programme doesn't do.** Teaching That Lands is three 90-minute sessions for people who teach. It covers slides, active learning, outcomes, evidence and follow-through. It doesn't make anyone an education lead, and it doesn't cover every row here. Where a row isn't covered and it matters for your teaching, involve someone who knows that area. Don't skip the row.

## How to use it

Mark each row **✓** ready, **~** partly, **✗** not yet or **N/A**, and write one line on *where's the evidence?* A tick without a note doesn't count.

Each row also says when to tackle it (**Start**, **Next** or **Then**: see below) and how much of it Teaching That Lands covers. Topics in *italics* don't have a library page yet.

## Lots of gaps? Start here

Most organisations find plenty of gaps. That's normal. Don't try to fix everything at once: pick one session and one row this quarter.

1. **Start: three questions on every request.** What should people do differently (rows 1 and 4)? How will we know they can (row 4)? Who will ask them about it at 30 days (row 10)? It costs almost nothing, it filters out requests that aren't teaching problems, and it brings managers in.
2. **Next: better sessions.** Learners, a second pair of eyes, practice and slides (rows 2, 5, 6 and 7). This is what [the three sessions](/sessions) teach, so running a cohort is the next step.
3. **Then: the system.** Co-production, inclusion, assessment and keeping it alive (rows 3, 8, 9 and 11). This needs an education lead. Basic accessibility can't wait, though: captions, and asking about adjustments beforehand, are legal duties now.

**Your list of gaps is evidence.** If you don't have an education lead, the gaps are the case for one.

## Before

### 1. Is teaching the answer?

*Start · Touched on: [Before You Plan](/facilitator/before-you-plan), question 2*

**What should people do differently, what would show it (ideally something you already measure), and why don't they do it now?**

- Would they do it if their job depended on it? If so, the problem isn't skill: look at kit, staffing, workload, the form or the culture.
- Would a job aid, a checklist or a change to the process do the job instead?
- What will it cost? People × hours, plus travel and backfill. What else could those hours buy?
- → *Is training the answer?*

**Red flags:** the request arrives as a format ("a study day on X"). The date is set before the problem is.

### 2. Learners and context

*Next · Touched on: it's on the design canvas, but not practised*

**Who exactly, how many, and what can they already do?**

- What they've been taught before, and what was tried before.
- Nights, part-time and bank staff. Staff educated overseas, and English as an additional language. Neurodivergent learners.
- Do they know why it matters to them? Are they choosing to come, or sent? → [Andragogy](/theories/how-adults-learn) · [Zone of Proximal Development](/theories/just-enough-help)
- Do some groups do less well under the current approach? → *Attainment gaps*

**Red flags:** "all staff". Nobody has spoken to a learner. Mandatory for everyone, whatever they can already do.

### 3. Co-production

*Then · Not covered*

**Who shaped this, apart from the person who asked for it?**

- Two or three of the learners, and their managers.
- Patients or carers where care is affected. Budget for their time and expenses.
- Whoever will deliver it.
- Something that already exists, rather than building it again.
- → *Co-production*

**Red flags:** designed in a meeting with no learners in it. A patient story used as decoration, not as a voice.

### 4. Outcomes

*Start · Covered: [Session 3](/facilitator/session-3)*

**One to three things people will be able to *do*, with verbs you could watch?**

- Which level of Miller's pyramid does each outcome reach? Can a "does" outcome be reached in one session? → [Miller's Pyramid](/theories/knowing-isnt-doing)
- Some ideas take time to click. → [Threshold Concepts](/theories/ideas-that-change-how-you-see)

**Red flags:** "understand", "be aware of", "appreciate". Ten outcomes for an hour.

### 5. Who's teaching it?

*Next · Not covered*

**Who will deliver it, are they prepared and supported, and who other than the designer has checked the design?**

- Protected time to prepare. Do they know the subject *and* how to teach it?
- A colleague to observe and give feedback. → [Train the Trainer](/facilitator/train-the-trainer)
- Their own development, including Fellowship. → [Portfolio Mapping](/facilitator/portfolio-mapping)

**Red flags:** the subject expert is told to "do a session" with no time to prepare. The person who designed it is the only one who checked it.

## During

### 6. Format and activities

*Next · Covered: [Session 2](/facilitator/session-2), the activities. Not covered: choosing the format*

**Is a session the right shape? Will learners practise the outcome, somewhere it's safe to get it wrong?**

- A huddle, simulation, on-the-job teaching, e-learning or a job aid? Short sessions spread out, or one day? → [Spaced Practice](/theories/spread-it-out)
- How far up the active scale does the setting allow? → [ICAP](/theories/active-is-a-scale)
- Realistic practice, with room for mistakes. A briefing before and a debrief after. → [Psychological Safety](/theories/safe-to-speak-up) · [Debriefing](/theories/debriefing-with-curiosity)
- Messy, complex practice? → [Action Learning](/theories/learning-with-peers). Other professions in the room?
- Games: a quiz works because of retrieval, not because of the points. → *Gamification*

**Red flags:** the agenda is a list of speakers. "E-learning, so it's done." A game nobody can link to an outcome.

### 7. Slides and materials

*Next · Covered: [Session 1](/facilitator/session-1)*

**Does every slide earn its place? Is the clinical content right and up to date?**

- [Cognitive Load](/theories/too-much-at-once) and [Dual Coding](/theories/pictures-and-words). A one-page job aid instead of printed slides?
- Checked against current local policy and national guidance by whoever owns that guidance.
- Permission to use every image.

**Red flags:** 60 slides for an hour. The slides are the handout. Nobody knows which version of the policy they teach.

### 8. Accessible and inclusive

*Then, but the basics now · Touched on: [Session 1](/facilitator/session-1), slides only*

**Can everyone take part fully, and benefit equally?**

- Adjustments asked about beforehand. Plain language, captions and transcripts, dyslexia-friendly materials. E-learning that meets WCAG 2.1 AA.
- Timing for night staff, and the cost of attending.
- An equality impact assessment, if your organisation uses them.
- → *Design for everyone* · *Attainment gaps*

**Red flags:** "tell us on the day if you need anything". A video with no captions.

### 9. Assessment, if needed

*Then · Touched on: [Session 3](/facilitator/session-3), evidence. Not covered: formal assessment*

**Does it need assessing? If so, does the check match the outcome, and is it fair?**

- Is the check there to help people learn, or to sign them off? → *Assessment for learning* · [Feedback](/theories/feedback-people-can-use)
- Who owns the sign-off, and are they prepared to assess?
- Do some groups do worse on it? → *Attainment gaps*

**Red flags:** a quiz used to sign off a "does" outcome. A sign-off with nobody prepared to assess.

## After

### 10. Back at work

*Start · Covered: [Session 3](/facilitator/session-3), after the session. Touched on: before it*

**Will the workplace let people use it, soon, and keep them to it?**

- **Before:** managers know what's being taught and what they'll be asked to support. Learners know why they're going.
- **After:** a chance to use it within days. Something to revisit at 30, 60 and 90 days. A named person, and the support they've agreed.
- → *Back at work* · [Implementation Intentions](/theories/if-then-plans) · [Kirkpatrick](/theories/did-the-training-work) · [Evaluation Toolkit](/facilitator/evaluation-toolkit)

**Red flags:** success means attendance and smile sheets. "We'll email the slides." Managers hear about it afterwards, if at all.

### 11. Keep it alive

*Then · Not covered*

**Who owns it, how will it improve, and when is it next reviewed?**

- Run it once with a small group before rolling it out.
- Evaluate *the session*, not only the learners, and change it.
- A named content owner and a review date. Who takes it over if they leave?

**Red flags:** the same slides for five years. No review date. The only person who knows the plan is moving on.
